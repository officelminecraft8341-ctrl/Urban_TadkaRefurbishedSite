# Owner-review checklist — Urban Tadka website concept

For agency use, in discussion with the restaurant owner. This document — not the
public website — is where every open question lives. Nothing here is shown to
diners. Each item says what is needed, why it matters, and what the owner must
supply or approve.

Status labels used below:

- **Needs owner confirmation** — a fact on the site that must be checked.
- **Photography needed** — no usable image exists yet.
- **Awaiting approved copy** — wording must come from the owner.
- **Verified** — confirmed against an authoritative source; no action needed.
- **Not applicable** — the owner has decided against it; recorded so it is not
  asked about twice.

Last reviewed: 15 October 2026, against the preview build then live.

On-site mirror: each photo panel on the site names the shot it is holding the
place for, in the middle of the panel itself. When a photograph is shot and
swapped in, its line in section 1 below is marked done.

---

## 1. Photography — the single biggest gap

**Status: Photography needed.** No photographs are shipped with this concept. Every
image area is an art panel holding its place. Using the two photographs on the
restaurant's current website is not an option: they read as generic stock, and we
hold no written permission to redistribute them. Generated imagery is out of the
question — it must never stand in for real food.

What we need from the owner:

1. **Written permission** to photograph the premises, the food and the staff, and
   to publish the resulting images on the restaurant's website. Without this,
   nothing on this list can be used.
2. **The photographs below**, feasible on a phone in a single session — no studio,
   no specialist equipment:
   - Dining room during dinner service, wide.
   - Exterior on Market Place Blvd, daytime.
   - Chef lifting naan out of the tandoor.
   - Biryani served in its handi, lid opened at the table.
   - A table set with three curries, rice and a naan basket.
   - Samosas plated with chutney, close-up.
   - Tandoori platter arriving at a table.
   - Whole spices in a steel bowl, close-up.
   - Lassi in a tall glass.
   - Gulab jamun in syrup, two pieces in a small bowl.
   - A full table of dishes, shot from directly above.
   - Counter and pickup area, staffed.
   - Hero spread of dishes on the table, shot from above in warm light.
   - Hands adding whole spices to a hot pan (the tadka technique).
   - Dish close-ups: Samosa, Butter Chicken, Tandoori Chicken, Gulab Jamun.
3. **How to shoot them:** daylight where possible, never mixed with overhead
   fluorescents in the same frame; food at 45° or straight down, never with the
   phone's flash; wipe the rim of the plate; landscape 4:3 for every shot; include
   the room and the tandoor, not just the food; written agreement from anyone
   recognisable in frame; full-resolution originals, unedited.
4. **Photo decisions already made by analysis** (kept here so they are not lost):
   the green herb-coated kebabs and the sizzler platter are worth shooting —
   see `05-image-identification.md`.

Why it matters: this is the difference between a concept and a launch. Every panel
on the site is sized and positioned for its photograph already.

## 2. About-page copy and restaurant history

**Status: Awaiting approved copy** for anything beyond what is on the page.

The About page as built uses only the restaurant's own published description of
its cooking (the undivided Punjab, the named cities, the meaning of tadka, the
spice list). That is verified and needs no action beyond confirming it still
reflects how the owner wants to be described.

What we need from the owner, if they want it included:

- A short history in their own words — founding year, how the restaurant started.
- Whether to name any people, and the exact wording they approve.
- Any press coverage they want quoted, with the source and date.
- A decision: no history at all is also an answer. The page works without it,
  and nothing will be invented either way.

## 3. Menu and culinary descriptions

**Status: Needs owner confirmation.** Dish names, descriptions and prices are
transcribed verbatim from the restaurant's own menu page (last updated 2019).
The site adds nothing and rewrites nothing — which means it also reproduces the
source's open questions:

- **Fourteen dish names appear twice at different prices.** Both entries are shown
  exactly as published. The owner must rule on each: Chicken Biryani ($12.99 /
  $15.99), Chilli Paneer ($13.99 / $18.49), Kadai Paneer ($10.99 / $18.49), Lamb
  Biryani ($13.99 / $18.99), Lassi Punjabi ($1.99 / $3.99), Mango Lassi ($2.99 /
  $4.99), Masala Chai ($1.99 / $2.99), Masala Gucchi ($10.99 / $17.49), Patiala
  Lassi ($2.99 / $5.99), Saag Paneer ($10.99 / $18.49), Shrimp Biryani ($14.95 /
  $18.99), Smokey Baigan ($10.99 / $17.99), Soft Drinks ($0.99 / $2.50), Vegetable
  Biryani ($10.99 / $14.99). Possible causes are portion sizes, lunch versus
  dinner pricing, or a stale page — the owner knows which.
- **Current prices.** Everything shown is 2019 pricing until re-confirmed against
  the live kitchen menu.
- **Thin categories** — are these complete? Seafood (2 dishes), Tandoor (8),
  Sides (7).
- **Dietary and allergens.** The published menu states no vegetarian, vegan, halal,
  gluten-free or nut-free status and lists no allergens. The site deliberately
  shows no labels. If the kitchen wants any published, we need the exact wording —
  this affects people's health and cannot be guessed at.
- **Catering terms:** minimum and maximum headcount, notice required, delivery
  radius or pickup-only, pricing model, who receives enquiries and by which
  channel, which dishes scale to large orders.
- **Reservations:** taken, or walk-in only?
- **Lunch buffet:** still offered? Times and price?
- **Seasonal or rotating dishes** that should be marked as such.
